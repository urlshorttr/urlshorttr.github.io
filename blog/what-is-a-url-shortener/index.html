What Is a URL Shortener and When Should You Use One?

October 10, 2026 · 2 min read

A URL shortener is a service that takes a long web address and gives you a much shorter one. When someone opens the short address, the service sends their browser on to the original page.

How it works

The service keeps a small record: the short ending (for example abc123) and the full address it stands for. When a browser asks for the short link, the service answers with a redirect that says "go to this other address instead". The visitor lands on the real page within a moment.

When a short link helps

  • Social profiles and captions. Space is limited and a long address looks messy.
  • Printed material. A short link is easier to type by hand, and it makes a simpler QR code.
  • Spoken or on-screen links. In a talk, a video or a podcast, people can remember or type a short address.
  • Messy addresses. Links from shops and search results often carry long strings of tracking parameters.

When it does not help

  • Links that must look trustworthy at a glance. A short link hides the destination until it is opened, so some readers will hesitate. See how to check a short link.
  • Private pages. Anyone who has the short link can open it.
  • Pages you may move. A short link cannot normally be edited after it is made.

Good habits

  1. Check the original address before you shorten it. A typo gets copied into the short link.
  2. Keep the original somewhere safe, in case you need to make the link again.
  3. Test the short link in a private window.
  4. If you print it, add a QR code and scan it with a phone first.
