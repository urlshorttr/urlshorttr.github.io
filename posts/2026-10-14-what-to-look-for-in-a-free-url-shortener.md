Dozens of websites will shorten a link for free. They look alike on the surface: a box, a button and a short address at the end. The differences show up later, when a link stops working, when a visitor sees a warning page, or when you find out what the service did with your data. This checklist helps you judge a free URL shortener before you rely on it.

## 1. It should be quick and need no account

Shortening a link is a ten-second job. If a service asks you to register, confirm an email address and click through a tour before it will give you a short URL, it is built to collect your details, not to help you. A good tool lets you paste the address, press one button and copy the result. Speed matters on the visitor's side too. The redirect should happen almost instantly, with no waiting page in between.

## 2. It should say what it stores

A short link only works because the service remembers your original address. That is unavoidable, but a trustworthy service says so in plain words. Look for a privacy policy that explains what is stored (the address and its short ending), whether clicks are counted and whether any account or profile is created. If you cannot find a privacy page at all, treat that as a warning sign.

## 3. It should let you choose the ending

A random ending such as `x7Tq2a` works, but a readable one is easier to trust and to remember. Being able to choose something like `spring_sale` makes a short link look like it belongs to you. Check that the tool tells you clearly when a name is already taken instead of failing silently. You can read more in our guide to [choosing a custom short link ending](/blog/how-to-choose-a-custom-short-link-ending/).

## 4. It should show where a link goes

Short links hide their destination, which is exactly what scammers like. A responsible shortener gives visitors a way to look before they leave, such as a preview page. On this site you can add a plus sign (`+`) to the end of any short link, or use the [link checker](/check-short-link/). A service that offers no preview and no way to report abuse is easy for bad actors to misuse, and your own links then share a reputation with theirs.

## 5. It should deal with abuse

Free shorteners attract spam. The ones worth using remove harmful links when they are reported, limit how many links one connection can create and refuse to shorten links that point at other redirect services. Look for an abuse or report page. If a shortener never removes anything, the domain will end up on blocklists, and then email programs and browsers may flag every link on it, including yours.

## 6. It should be honest about permanence

No free service can promise to run forever. What you want is honesty. A good shortener says whether links expire, what happens if the service closes and whether links can be edited afterwards. Keep your original address somewhere safe, particularly for printed material. If a link is printed on thousands of leaflets, you should be able to recover from a service change.

## 7. It should work well on a phone

Most short links are opened and created on phones. Check that the page loads quickly on a mobile connection, that the copy button works and that the text is readable without zooming. A QR code option is a useful extra, because it lets you move a link from a screen to a printed page.

## 8. It should not be cluttered with pop-ups

Ads are how most free tools are paid for, and that is fair. But ads that cover the button, move the page while you read or trick you into clicking are a red flag. A page that is usable first and monetised second is a sign the owners care about visitors.

## A quick test you can run in a minute

1. Open the site on your phone and time how long the page takes to become usable.
2. Shorten a harmless link, such as a page you own.
3. Add a custom ending, then try a name that is almost certainly taken, and see how the error reads.
4. Open the short link in a private window and check it redirects without extra steps.
5. Find the privacy policy and the abuse report page.

If a service passes all five, it is a good candidate. If it fails more than one, pick another. When you are ready, you can [shorten a link here](/) with a custom ending and download its QR code in the same step.
