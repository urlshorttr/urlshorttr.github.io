When you shorten a link you hand a web address to a service and get a shorter one back. It is worth knowing what happens to that address, who can see it and what a shortener can and cannot do for your privacy.

## What a shortener has to store

A short link works because the service keeps a list. Next to each short ending sits the long address it should send people to. When someone opens the short link, the service looks up the ending and sends the visitor on. So at a minimum, every shortener stores the long address you gave it. This is not a fault. It is how the service works.

## What a shortener may also collect

Beyond that, services differ. Some count clicks, record the country or device of each visitor, or ask you to create an account. Others do very little. Before you use one, look for a privacy policy that says plainly:

- what is stored about the links;
- whether visits are counted or logged;
- whether an account or profile is created;
- how long data is kept;
- how to ask for a link to be removed.

On this site the address and short ending are stored, no accounts exist and only a click total is kept for each link, with no IP addresses or personal details. To limit abuse, the service temporarily counts how many links a single connection creates in an hour. Our [privacy policy](/privacy/) has the details.

## A short link is not a private link

This is the most important point. Anyone who has a short link can open it. A short ending is not a password. Endings can be guessed, particularly if they are short, and a link you shared once can be forwarded without you knowing. Never shorten a link to something that must stay private, such as a personal document, a medical record, a private photo album or a page with an account in it, unless access to that page is protected by its own login.

If you need to share a private file, use the sharing controls of the service that holds it, set it to specific people and, where possible, give it an expiry date.

## The destination page matters more than the short link

The page the link leads to decides what visitors see and which cookies or trackers it uses. Shortening does not change that. If the destination collects data, it will still do so after a visitor arrives from your short link.

## Tracking parameters

Many long addresses carry parameters such as `utm_source=` that tell the site where a visitor came from. They are used for marketing statistics. A short link can hide them from the person reading the address, but they still travel with the visitor to the destination page. If you would rather not pass them on, remove the parameters before shortening.

## What your visitors can learn from a short link

A visitor can see the short address, and after the redirect, the full address in their browser. If your long address includes personal information, such as a name, email address, order number or token, every visitor can see it. Keep personal data out of web addresses, and do not shorten links that contain secret tokens.

## Recent links are stored in your own browser

The tool on this site keeps a short list of your recent links in your browser, so you can find them again. It stays on your device and is never sent to us. You can clear it with the "Clear list" button on the [home page](/), or by clearing site data in your browser settings. If you share a computer, clear it when you finish.

## Choosing a shortener that respects you

- It explains what it stores in words you understand.
- It has no account requirement for basic use.
- It offers a way to preview and to report links.
- It has a contact address that answers.
- It does not show deceptive pop-ups.

Our guide to [what to look for in a free URL shortener](/blog/what-to-look-for-in-a-free-url-shortener/) goes through the full checklist.

## Removing a link

If a short link should no longer exist, ask the service to remove it. On this site, write to the contact address on the [contact page](/contact/) with the short link and a brief explanation. Remember that anyone who already has the link, or who has saved the destination, may still know where it pointed.
