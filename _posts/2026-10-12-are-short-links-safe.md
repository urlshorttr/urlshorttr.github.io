A short link is not unsafe by itself. It is a normal link with a shorter address. The concern is that you cannot see the destination before you open it, and that is something a careless or dishonest sender can take advantage of.

## Preview before you open

On URLShort you can add a plus sign (`+`) to the end of any short link, for example `urlshort.tr/abc123+`. A page then shows the full destination and lets you decide whether to continue. Other services have their own ways to preview a link, so check how the one you are using works.

## Signs to be careful

- A message you did not expect that asks you to log in, pay or confirm something.
- A sense of urgency, such as "your account will be closed today".
- A preview that shows an address that does not match the company the message claims to be from.
- A destination that asks for passwords, card numbers or one-time codes.

## What to do instead of clicking

1. Preview the link, as above.
2. Go to the company&rsquo;s website by typing its address yourself.
3. Ask the sender through another channel if you are not sure.

## If you share short links

- Say what the link leads to in the text around it.
- Use a custom ending when you can, so the link looks intentional.
- Do not shorten pages with private information. Anyone with the link can open it.
